# Ethics, Authorization, and Professional Conduct

Penetration testing must be performed only with appropriate
authorization and within the agreed scope.

## Core Ethical Principles

### Do No Harm

Testing should minimize unnecessary disruption, data exposure, and
operational risk.

### Confidentiality

Information discovered during an engagement must be protected and
handled according to the agreed requirements.

### Legal Authorization

A tester must have permission before testing systems, applications,
networks, people, or physical locations.

## Written Authorization

Important engagement documents can include:

- Master Services Agreement (MSA)
- Statement of Work (SoW)
- Rules of Engagement (RoE)

These documents establish what is being tested, what activities are
permitted, and the responsibilities of the parties involved.

## Scope Must Be Respected

The tester must understand:

- In-scope assets
- Out-of-scope assets
- Permitted techniques
- Testing windows
- Restrictions
- Emergency contacts
- Data-handling requirements

> Authorization for one system does not automatically authorize testing
> another system.

## Handling Mistakes

If testing causes an unexpected problem:

1.  Stop or reduce the activity if necessary.
2.  Follow the agreed communication procedure.
3.  Inform the appropriate contact.
4.  Document what happened.
5.  Follow the engagement’s recovery or escalation process.

## Data Handling and Privacy

### Data Extraction

Only collect information necessary for the authorized assessment.

### Data Security

Protect collected evidence and sensitive information.

### Data Disposal

Dispose of assessment data according to the engagement requirements.

### Personal Use

Do not use client data for personal purposes.

### Legal Compliance

Follow applicable laws, regulations, contracts, and organizational
policies.

## Documentation and Reporting

Good documentation should explain:

- What was tested.
- What was observed.
- What evidence was collected.
- What impact was identified.
- How the issue can be remediated.

## Social Engineering Considerations

Social-engineering testing requires especially clear authorization and
controlled scenarios.

### Support Channels

Testers should know the agreed contacts and escalation paths before
conducting social-engineering activities.

### Risks of Unethical Social Engineering

Unauthorized or poorly controlled social engineering can cause:

- Privacy violations
- Business disruption
- Loss of trust
- Legal problems

## Practical Ethical Checklist

Before testing, confirm:

- [ ] Written authorization exists.
- [ ] Scope is clearly defined.
- [ ] Testing windows are known.
- [ ] Out-of-scope assets are identified.
- [ ] Emergency contacts are available.
- [ ] Data-handling requirements are understood.
- [ ] Rules of engagement are understood.
- [ ] Evidence will be stored securely.

## Key Takeaway

Ethical penetration testing is not simply about having permission to run
tools. It requires professional judgment, scope control,
confidentiality, careful documentation, and responsible handling of
discovered information.
